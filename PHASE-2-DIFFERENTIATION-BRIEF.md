**How to use this:** under each page there are three blocks:

- **Questions for you** — your expertise, experience, or opinion. Answering these

- **Research to run** — facts/numbers to look up (or ask a research system). Each

## Why these pages aren't indexed (the root cause)

The 6 industry pages are 85% the same page with a different noun. Same section
order, same FAQ shape ("Wie werde ich in ChatGPT sichtbar?", "Wie schnell wirkt
es?", "Was kostet es?"), same stock claims (YMYL, E-E-A-T, NAP, Schema.org,
"2-3 Empfehlungen"). Google treats a cluster like this as **scaled content
abuse** and declines to index the weaker siblings. No amount of internal linking
fixes a duplication problem; the content itself has to diverge.

The fix has two layers:
1. **Structural divergence** — each page gets a *different dominant section* that
   reflects how that vertical actually buys, not the same 7-strategy skeleton.
2. **Proprietary substance** — first-hand experience, a real local example, and
   vertical-specific numbers/tools that physically cannot appear on a sibling page.

## The recipe every Phase 2 page must hit ("really perfect")

Blend of all three skills, in priority order for these pages (ChatGPT-via-Bing and
Perplexity are the engines that answer "recommend me a [provider]" queries):

1. **Define every key term, attach a number.** (Bing's #1 lever, proven on
   `/wissen/ki-sichtbarkeit-messen` = 60% of this site's citations.) Each important
   concept gets a heading + one-sentence definition + a figure. "YMYL" is not a
   citable token until it's defined with a stake attached.
2. **Name real, branded tools — never categories.** "Jameda", "Doctolib",
   "anwalt.de", "MyHammer", "ImmoScout24", "GPTBot", "PerplexityBot", "Schema.org
   MedicalClinic" are retrievable anchors. "Ein Bewertungsportal" is not.
3. **A number on every claim.** Price, range, %, count, timeframe. "ab X €/Monat",
   "2-3 Empfehlungen", "40% der unter 35-Jährigen". Adjectives get ignored; benchmarks
   get quoted.
4. **First-hand experience, visible authorship.** (Google E-E-A-T.) One real thing
   you saw, did, or measured. A byline that links to credentials. This is the single
   biggest lever for *indexing*, and it's the part only you can write.
5. **One unique asset per page** — a table, checklist, or sample-prompt block that
   no sibling repeats. Comparison tables are the single most-cited content type (~33%).
6. **40-60 word self-contained answer blocks** under question-shaped headings.
7. **Query-shaped, vertical-specific FAQ.** Kill the generic 7. Replace with
   questions only someone in *that* vertical asks (see per-page lists below).
8. **Humanizer pass + em-dash removal** as you touch each page (Phase 3, folded in).

A note on the Google/Bing split (per our standing finding): Google officially says
schema, llms.txt, and AI-keyword phrasing don't help *its* AI features, while Bing's
data shows term density and named tools are decisive. We keep both true: write for
humans + E-E-A-T (Google indexes you), and layer dense defined jargon + numbers
(Bing/Copilot cites you). They don't conflict on the same page.
